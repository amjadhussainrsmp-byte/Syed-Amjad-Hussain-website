Historical Profile

Hazrat Makhdooma Bibi Kamaal

Hazrat Makhdooma Bibi Kamal of Kako was a 13th-century Sufi saint of Bihar, revered as one of India’s earliest prominent female Sufi figures and remembered for spiritual teaching, healing, and a legacy of religious tolerance.

Biography

Hazrat Bibi Kamaal is traditionally identified as Syeda Bibi Hadiya (or Hadia), daughter of Qazi Syed Shahabuddin Suhrawardy “Pir Jagjot” and Bibi Noor, also called Malka-e-Jahan. Accounts differ on her birth date and place: one widely repeated tradition places her birth in 1211 CE in Kashgar, while other local traditions date it around 1174 CE or approximately 1204 CE; this uncertainty is important because her early life rests largely on hagiographic and genealogical traditions rather than securely dated contemporary records. She is said to have married Shaikh Sulaiman Langar Zameen, a Sufi associated with Maner Sharif, and to have settled at Kako, near present-day Jehanabad in Bihar. She had a son, Shah Ataullah, and a daughter, Bibi Daulat; the latter married Makhdum Husamuddin Hansi Hansarvi, linking Bibi Kamal’s family to several influential Sufi lineages of eastern India.

Chronology & Historical Context

Hazrat Bibi Kamal is traditionally said to have arrived in Kako in 1274 CE, accompanied by her daughter and household, during a period when Sufi institutions were becoming deeply rooted in Bihar. Her lifetime overlapped with the consolidation of Muslim political and religious influence in the region following the late-12th-century conquests associated with Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji, although the precise relationship between political history and her settlement at Kako is not firmly documented. Her death date is likewise uncertain: modern accounts often give 1296 CE, while a biographical account published on Sufinama, places it broadly between 1290 and 1300 CE. Her annual urs is observed in late Bhadon (the local calendar month), and the shrine’s major public commemoration is now held in September or October.

Spiritual Lineage & Teachings

Hazrat Bibi Kamal is traditionally connected with the Suhrawardi Sufi orfer through her father, Pir Jagjot, and through her husband’s family at Maner Sharif. She is also described as the maternal aunt of Hazrat Shaykh Sharfuddin Ahmed Yahya Maneri, the celebrated Sufi of Bihar Sharif, although the exact kinship and its historical verification remain matters of tradition. Her remembered teaching emphasized compassion, service to humanity, healing, and resistance to religious orthodoxy and social exclusiveness. She is portrayed as a spiritual guide whose khanqah welcomed seekers irrespective of religion or social status; this inclusive reputation is central to why she is celebrated as a symbol of communal harmony in Bihar.

Khanqah, Dargah & Regional Legacy

At Kako, Hazrat Bibi Kamal is said to have established a spiritual retreat or khanqah that became a centre for counsel, care for the sick, and devotional gathering. Her shrine—known as Hazrat Bibi Kamal ka Maqbara or Kako Dargah—stands about 8 km east of Jehanabad town and is presented by Bihar Tourism as India’s first dargah dedicated to a woman Sufi saint. The shrine complex includes the Sehat Kuan (“Well of Health”), associated in local tradition with healing, especially for skin and chronic ailments. It also contains sacred stones known as Kaarah and Nayan Katori, which devotees associate with spiritual relief and improved eyesight respectively. Sultan Firoz Shah Tughlaq is traditionally said to have visited Kako in 1359 CE while travelling from Delhi to Bihar Sharif, and to have ordered the construction or improvement of Bibi Kamal’s tomb and an Eidgah. Later traditions also associate Sher Shah Suri and Mughal princess Jahanara Begum with offerings at the shrine, reflecting its long-standing regional prestige.

Works, Manuscripts & Related Figures

No securely authenticated corpus of writings by Hazrat Bibi Kamal herself appears in the available historical record; her legacy is transmitted chiefly through oral tradition, shrine practices, genealogies, and later Sufi biographical writing. The article by Raiyan Abulolai on Sufinama account explicitly notes that detailed, well-attested records of her life and teachings have been difficult to recover, and that fires in Kako damaged older records and manuscripts. Her spiritual and familial network is nevertheless important: her husband Hazrat Shaikh Sulaiman Langar Zameen, her son Shah Ataullah, her daughter Bibi Daulat, her son-in-law Makhdoom Husamuddin, and her nephew Makhdoom Sharfuddin Ahmed Yahya Maneri all belong to the wider Sufi landscape of Bihar. The later Sajjadiyya–Abulolaiyya family of Danapur also traces its spiritual ancestry to this household, illustrating how Bibi Kamal’s legacy continued through regional Sufi lineages.
