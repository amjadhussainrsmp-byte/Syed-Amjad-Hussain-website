Biography

Hazrat Makhdoom Adam Sufi (1187–1297), popularly known as Adam Sufi, was a prominent Islamic scholar, preacher, and Sufi saint associated with the Chishti order of Sufism in the Indian subcontinent. He holds a place in the spiritual history of Bihar, particularly in the region surrounding present-day Patna. Adam Sufi was the son of Sayyid Ibrahim Chishti of Hajipur and the grandson of Khwaja Sayyid Jalal al-Din Abdal Chishti al-Mashhadi, who is traditionally associated with the spiritual lineage of the Chishti order. According to genealogical accounts cited in historical literature, his ancestry traces back to Imam Zayn al-Abidin, the great-grandson of Prophet Muhammad. Makhdoom Adam Sufi was the father of Sayyid Hamid al-Din Chishti Rajgiri and the grandfather of Taym Allah Safayd Baz. His family lineage reflects the enduring connection between scholarly learning, spiritual traditions, and Sufi networks in medieval Bihar. His final resting place is Pakki Dargah, near Jethuli in Patna district, Bihar, India. The shrine forms part of the region's wider landscape of Islamic and Sufi heritage. Makhdoom Adam Sufi remains a notable figure in the recorded history of the Chishti tradition in Bihar, representing the region's longstanding spiritual and religious legacy.

Chronology & Historical Context

Makhdoom Adam Sufi is traditionally dated to 1187–1297 CE and is associated with the Chishti Sufi tradition in medieval Bihar. His life falls within a period when Sufi orders were expanding across the Indian subcontinent, establishing spiritual networks that connected religious scholars, disciples, and local communities. According to biographical accounts, Adam Sufi was the son of Sayyid Ibrahim Chishti of Hajipur and the grandson of Khwaja Sayyid Jalal al-Din Abdal Chishti al-Mashhadi. His traditional dates span the late twelfth and thirteenth centuries, although detailed contemporary documentation of his life has not been established here.

Spiritual Lineage & Teachings

Adam Sufi is identified in biographical literature as belonging to the Chishti Sufi tradition. His family genealogy traces his ancestry through Shah Ibrahim Chishti and Khwaja Sayyid Jalal al-Din Abdal Chishti al-Mashhadi, with the lineage further connected to Imam Zayn al-Abidin. The Chishti tradition historically emphasised spiritual discipline, devotion, humility, service, and the relationship between a spiritual guide and disciples.

Khanqah, Dargah & Regional Legacy

Adam Sufi's tomb is identified as being at Pakki Dargah, near Jethuli in Patna district, Bihar, India. The shrine connects his memory with the region's wider landscape of Sufi heritage. Dargahs have historically served as places of visitation and remembrance, while khanqahs functioned as centres for spiritual instruction, communal gatherings, hospitality, and religious learning. Nevertheless, specific claims about the establishment of a khanqah by Adam Sufi, its architectural history, annual observances, or historical patronage require independent evidence.
