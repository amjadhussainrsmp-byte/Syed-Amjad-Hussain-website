Sheikhpura: A History of People, Place and Faith

A growing historical guide to Sheikhpura district—its Sufi traditions, sacred places, public life and the personalities whose stories form part of the region’s shared heritage.

The district at a glance

A small district with a layered past

Situated in south Bihar and now part of Munger Division, Sheikhpura has a history connected with older administrative regions, local settlement traditions, religious institutions, agriculture and the stone-working landscape around its rocky hills. The district took its present administrative identity on 31 July 1994, when it was separated from the former Munger district.

The region’s heritage is not contained in a single story. It includes oral traditions associated with Girihinda, the Sufi memory attached to Makhdoom Shaikh Shah Shoaib Firdausi, Hindu places of worship, the public legacy of Sri Krishna Singh and the lives of scholars, poets and social figures linked to towns and villages across the district.

This page distinguishes documented administrative dates from traditions that need further archival or archaeological verification. It is intended as a research resource, not a claim that every local tradition has been independently proven.

Historical outline

Chronology of Sheikhpura

A concise timeline based on official district information and regional historical traditions. Early periods require more primary-source research.

Epic tradition · undated

Girihinda and the Hidimba tradition

Local tradition connects the hill at Girihinda with Hidimba of the Mahabharata. The association is an important part of regional cultural memory, but it should be presented as tradition rather than as an archaeologically established date.

Medieval period

The growth of Sheikhpura settlement

The district administration records a local belief that the Sufi saint Makhdoom Shah Shoaib was associated with the founding and growth of Sheikhpura around six centuries ago. The details and chronology deserve examination through shrine records, manuscripts and older gazetteers.

Mughal period

An administrative thana

According to the district’s published historical account, Sheikhpura acquired the status of a thana during the Mughal period. The precise administrative record should be established through Persian documents and regional revenue records.

British period

Changing local administration

The district’s official history describes Sheikhpura as a “Big Kotwali” under British rule. Colonial gazetteers, settlement reports and police records are valuable leads for further research.

14 April 1983

Becomes a subdivision

Sheikhpura was made a subdivision before it became a separate district. This transition is a key milestone in the modern administrative history of the area.

31 July 1994

District of Sheikhpura established

Sheikhpura was carved out of the former Munger district and established as a separate district in Bihar. The formation of the district marks a major turning point in its contemporary political and administrative identity.

People who shaped the region

Notable Personalities of Sheikhpura

This initial directory includes historically significant people linked to Sheikhpura and its surrounding heritage landscape. It is not an exhaustive list; names and biographical details should be expanded as archival sources are located.

Sufi saint · Firdausiyya

Makhdoom Shaikh Shah Shoaib Firdausi Sheikhpuri

A central figure in Sheikhpura’s Sufi memory, associated with the Firdausi tradition and Badi Dargah. Local and district histories connect him with the early growth of Sheikhpura. His life, lineage and dates merit careful study of shrine records and Persian and Urdu sources.

Associated place: Badi Dargah, Sheikhpura
Statesman · First Chief Minister of Bihar

Dr Sri Krishna Singh (Sri Babu)

Bihar’s first Chief Minister, who served from 1946/1947 until 1961, was born at Maur in the present-day Barbigha area. He is remembered for his role in the state’s political development and for his association with education and public institutions.

Associated place: Maur, Barbigha area
Sufi saint · Poet

Syed Shah Tajuddin Shakir

A respected Sufi figure and poet associated with Pind Sharif. His memory forms part of the district’s devotional and literary heritage. Dates and details of his life should be checked against local shrine histories and surviving writings.

Associated place: Pind Sharif
Scholar · Teacher · Author

Mir Abdullah Bihari

Remembered in regional accounts as an Islamic scholar, teacher, writer and Sufi associated with the Jajneri family and Pathraita. Biographical accounts describe his traditional education and scholarly work; manuscripts and contemporary records would help establish a fuller bibliography and chronology.

Associated place: Pathraita and the wider Sheikhpura region
Sufi saint · Maghribi tradition

Hazrat Khwaja Ishaq Maghribi

The Sufi shrine at Matokhar Sharif is associated with Khwaja Ishaq Maghribi. Local devotional memory places him within the wider medieval Sufi landscape of Bihar. His dates, spiritual connections and historical context should be documented from reliable shrine and manuscript sources.

Associated place: Matokhar Sharif
Sufi heritage · Jajneri tradition

Syed Ahmad Jajneri

A name associated with the Jajneri Sufi and scholarly heritage of the region. The precise biographical identification, dates and links to particular shrines should be confirmed from genealogical records, inscriptions and local manuscripts before adding more definitive claims.

Associated heritage: Jajneri tradition in the region
Sufi saint · Regional shrine tradition

Shah Yusuf Jajneri (Shah Dulha)

Known in local Sufi heritage narratives by the name Shah Dulha, Shah Yusuf Jajneri is associated with Chondargah. The shrine’s oral history and documentary record offer an important subject for research into the movement of Sufi families and devotional networks in the region.

Associated place: Chondargah
Sufi and scholarly lineage

Malik Ibrahim Baya

A notable name in Bihar’s broader Sufi history and in regional discussions of older spiritual networks. His inclusion here reflects the importance of tracing connections between Sheikhpura and neighbouring centres; a direct local association should be stated only when supported by a specific source.

Regional context: Bihar’s Sufi history
Modern public life

Rajo Singh

A prominent political figure associated with the region’s modern political history. Secondary accounts connect his political efforts with the creation of Sheikhpura district. His career and the district-formation process can be explored through legislative records, contemporary newspapers and official notifications.

Places and living memory

Historical and Heritage Places

These places represent different layers of Sheikhpura’s cultural landscape. The page can later be expanded with photographs, maps, inscriptions, access details and source notes.

01

Girihinda Pahar

A prominent rocky hill and a major landmark of Sheikhpura town, known for its temple and the local tradition linking the place-name with Hidimba. The traditional account is culturally significant but should not be confused with conclusive archaeological evidence.

02

Badi Dargah

A significant Sufi shrine associated with Makhdoom Shaikh Shah Shoaib Firdausi. The shrine’s oral history, annual observances, inscriptions and records are important sources for understanding Sheikhpura’s Sufi past.

03

Matokhar Sharif

A well-known Sufi site associated with Khwaja Ishaq Maghribi. Its shrine and pond landscape are part of the region’s religious and cultural memory and deserve documentation through fieldwork and historical records.

04

Pind Sharif

A village known for its Sufi heritage, including the memory of Syed Shah Tajuddin Shakir. It offers a useful starting point for documenting local devotional poetry, shrine traditions and community histories.

05

Samas Vishnudham

Samas village is known for its Vishnu temple and an image of Vishnu. It forms an important part of Sheikhpura’s religious landscape and appears among the district’s recognised places of interest.

06

Dal Kuan

A historic well associated in local and district accounts with Sheikhpura’s past and with a tradition linking it to Sher Shah Suri. The precise date, patronage and original function require documentary verification.

07

Chondargah

A shrine location associated in local Sufi heritage narratives with Shah Yusuf Jajneri, also known as Shah Dulha. Its history can be developed through site photography, oral interviews and the study of shrine records.

08

Khariyapar Pahar

A hill area with a local religious tradition and temple sites. It contributes to the wider sacred geography of Sheikhpura and should be documented alongside the district’s other hill and temple landscapes.

09

Maur and Barbigha

The Maur–Barbigha area is historically notable as the birthplace region of Sri Krishna Singh. Its role in the district’s modern political history makes it relevant to a fuller biography and local-history archive.

How the past can be studied

Sources, Records and Further Research

A responsible local history should bring official records into conversation with manuscripts, inscriptions, oral testimony and on-site observation.

  • District Sheikhpura, Government of Bihar — About District: official administrative milestones and the district’s published account of local traditions.
  • District Sheikhpura, Government of Bihar — Places of Interest: recognised local heritage and tourist places.
  • Census of India 2011 — District Census Handbook, Sheikhpura: district profile and historical overview.
  • Bihar Tourism — Sheikhpura: official tourism context and selected destinations.
  • Primary materials to seek: Persian and Urdu manuscripts, shrine registers, waqf records, grave and mosque inscriptions, old revenue and settlement records, colonial gazetteers, district-formation notifications, local newspapers and oral histories gathered with permission.

Online official summaries are useful starting points, but they do not settle every question of medieval chronology or local etymology. A claim should be upgraded from “tradition says” to a firm historical statement only when the evidence supports it.
