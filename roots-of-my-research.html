An Ancestral Legacy of Faith, Learning and Historical Inquiry

A researcher's note

Every research journey has a beginning. Mine began not in an archive or library, but among the stories of my family and the places of my childhood in Sheikhpura, Bihar. Those memories gave me questions to pursue; research became the means through which I could examine them, preserve them and share them responsibly.

Chapter 01

The Beginning of a Research Journey

My journey as an independent researcher and writer began when I was around seventeen years old, while I was studying at SADN Convent School in Sheikhpura, Bihar. I was living in my hometown, Sheikhpura, surrounded by the stories, memories and traditions of my family. Long before I began writing books, I had developed a deep interest in the lives of my ancestors, the places they had inhabited, the institutions they had served, and the religious and intellectual traditions they had carried forward.

During my childhood, I would listen attentively to stories told about my great-grandparents and earlier generations. These recollections gradually developed into a lasting curiosity about history, genealogy and heritage. I began to understand that family memories, old places, forgotten personalities and inherited traditions could provide valuable starting points for historical investigation. I wanted to preserve these accounts so that future generations could learn about the people and experiences that shaped my background. This personal interest eventually became the foundation of my research and writing.

Chapter 02

My Ancestral Heritage

My family history is closely associated with several villages in present-day Sheikhpura and Lakhisarai districts of Bihar, particularly Jamwara, Pathraita and Urain, also known as Oren. The family tradition preserves accounts of religious scholars, Qur'an memorizers, teachers, land donors and community figures whose lives were connected with Islamic learning, education and local society.

One of the most prominent figures in our ancestral history was Hazrat Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari (rahmatullahi ta'ala alaih), the great-grandfather of my grandfather. He is remembered in our family tradition as a respected Islamic preacher and as the teacher and spiritual guide of Rao Abdul Rahman Khan of Mircha, Jamui. He married into a family in Urain, in present-day Lakhisarai district. His father-in-law was Mir Irshad Hussain, popularly known as Gango Babu, who is remembered as a prominent zamindar of his time.

According to our family genealogy, Mir Irshad Hussain's family descended from Hazrat Syed Shah Muhammad Yusuf Dulha Jajneri through Hazrat Syed Shah Muhammad Jaan Jajneri (rahmatullahi ta'ala alaih) of Urain. Our own ancestral line, meanwhile, is traced to Hazrat Syed Shah Jamaluddin Jajneri through Hazrat Mir Syed Niyaz Ali of Pathraita, Sheikhpura. These genealogical traditions form an important part of the family's inherited understanding of its religious and regional heritage.

Hazrat Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari had two brothers whose contributions are also remembered in our family history: Hazrat Mir Syed Sakhawat Hussain, who is buried at Pathraita Masjid, and Mir Syed Shuja'at Hussain, who is remembered as a donor of land for the mosque. According to family accounts, Mir Abdullah Bihari and Mir Syed Sakhawat Hussain received their education at Madrasa of Firangi Mahal and devoted themselves to Islamic learning and preaching.

The next important figure in this lineage was Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz (rahmatullahi ta'ala alaih), the son of Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari and the father of my grandfather. He was a Hafiz of the Holy Qur'an and studied at the madrasa associated with Khanqah-e-Maulaiya at Maulanagar, Surajgarha, in present-day Lakhisarai district. His childhood connection with Urain, located near Maulanagar, formed part of the geographical setting of this family history.

Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz married a daughter of Abdul Hakeem, son of Hazrat Mir Sarfaraz Hussain, who is remembered as the donor of land for Jamwara Masjid. The family had ancestral landholding connections in the surrounding locality. In keeping with this legacy, land associated with the family was dedicated as waqf and came to be known as Hafiz Syed Abdul Aziz Qabristan, situated near the entrance to Jamwara village, Sheikhpura, Bihar.

The family tradition describes a long geographical journey: from Jamwara to Pathraita, then to Urain following marriage and family ties, and eventually back to Jamwara after generations. This movement across villages is an important part of the story I hope to document and preserve through historical research.

My grandfather, Hafiz Dr. Syed Aashique Hussain, was the son of Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz. He married in Charyari, Sheikhpura, and settled in Jamwara, reconnecting the family with the village that holds such importance in our ancestral history. Like his father, he was a Hafiz of the Holy Qur'an and studied at the madrasa associated with Khanqah-e-Maulaiya, Maulanagar, Surajgarha, Lakhisarai. He also practised as a homoeopathic physician. He passed away at the age of sixty-three and was buried at Hafiz Syed Abdul Aziz Qabristan in Jamwara, near the grave of his mother.

My grandfather's life represented the intersection of religious learning, professional service and family responsibility. His descendants continued to contribute to journalism, education and public service. Among his children were Late Syed Ainul Huda, a journalist associated with The Times of India; Syed Sadique Hussain, a former employee of the Bihar State Madrasa Board; Late Syed Arif Hussain, a former teacher at Madrasa Jamia Habibia, Jamwara; Syed Shabbir Ahmad, also a former teacher at Madrasa Jamia Habibia; and my father, Syed Ahmad Hussain Azizi.

My father served as secretary of Madrasa Jamia Habibia, Jamwara, and founded Madrasatul Banat Azizul Uloom in Jamwara, Sheikhpura, where he also served as its headmaster. The institution was established in memory of Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz to promote education for girls. Today, my mother serves as its In-charge Headmistress. For me, this institution represents an enduring continuation of the family's commitment to education and community service.

Chapter 03

From Family Memories to Historical Research

The stories of my ancestors and the heritage of the places associated with them gradually inspired me to move beyond listening and remembering towards systematic research and writing. I became interested in recovering the histories of people whose contributions deserved greater recognition and in documenting the cultural, intellectual and religious traditions of Bihar.

Following repeated encouragement from people close to me, I began working on The Eternals of Bihar, a book centred on twenty-five Muslim leaders, scholars and other personalities associated with Bihar. The project strengthened my interest in recording individual lives within their wider historical and regional contexts.

I subsequently wrote Akhtar Orenvi: Bihar Mein Urdu Sahitya Ke Nirmata, focusing on Akhtar Orenvi, who was associated with Urain, a place whose stories had been familiar to me since childhood. After learning more about the subject's religious affiliation, I decided to withdraw the book from publication because I did not wish to promote a figure whose beliefs conflicted with my own religious convictions. This decision reflected a personal matter of faith and publishing choice; it did not diminish my interest in studying the literary and cultural history of Bihar.

Chapter 04

The Journey of Bihar Aur Sufivad

My next major undertaking emerged when friends encouraged me to research Sufism in Bihar. I accepted the challenge because the subject brought together several interests that had shaped my intellectual journey: religious scholarship, historical personalities, regional heritage, spiritual traditions and the preservation of knowledge.

The result was Bihar Aur Sufivad, a book devoted to exploring the history and heritage of Sufism in Bihar. Preparing the work required sustained reading, the examination of historical and literary material, and engagement with information concerning Sufi personalities, spiritual lineages and institutions. I wanted the book to contribute to the documentation of Bihar's Sufi heritage and make this history accessible to a wider readership.

Several friends supported the project during its research, preparation and promotion. Among them were Muhammad Zabir Ansari of Jamui, an international karate champion and the founder and chairman of Zenith Social Foundation; Syed Rushan Ali of Pind Sharif; Muhammad Abid Arman Hasan of Dhanbad; and Muhammad Farhan Raza Qadri of Deoghar. Their encouragement and assistance helped me take the work forward and bring it to the attention of readers interested in Bihar's religious and cultural history.

After completing the manuscript, I sought feedback from several individuals to improve its quality. Professor Dr Uzma Azhar Ali of Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi, was among those I consulted. Her suggestions helped me identify areas requiring correction and refinement. Following the incorporation of the recommended revisions and the completion of copy-editing, I approached Dr Muhammad Sajjad Alam Rizvi of Presidency University, Kolkata, to write the foreword. He kindly contributed the foreword to the book.

With these stages completed, Bihar Aur Sufivad was published by Rajmangal Prakashan in 2025. The book subsequently reached a wider readership and achieved national bestseller recognition, marking an important milestone in my journey as an author.

More than a year after its publication, the work remains an important part of my ongoing engagement with the history of Sufism in Bihar. I also believe that historical writing must be accompanied by efforts to bring younger generations closer to the intellectual and spiritual traditions of the past. In this spirit, I have organised multiple programmes under the theme of “Youth in Sufism”, with the aim of encouraging young people to learn about Sufi heritage, its historical personalities and its place in the cultural life of Bihar.

Chapter 05

Preserving the Past, Inspiring the Future

My research has grown out of a personal connection with history, but my purpose extends beyond documenting my own ancestry. I hope to contribute to a broader understanding of Bihar's historical, literary and spiritual heritage by recording the lives of scholars, writers, religious figures and community institutions whose stories deserve careful study.

The family narratives that first captured my attention taught me to value memory; research taught me to investigate it; and writing gave me a means of sharing what I discovered. I recognise that inherited accounts must be examined alongside documentary evidence, manuscripts, published works, inscriptions and other historical sources wherever they are available. Preserving family traditions responsibly means recording them while continuing to investigate their historical details.

From the stories I heard as a child in Sheikhpura to the research that culminated in The Eternals of Bihar and Bihar Aur Sufivad, my journey has been shaped by a continuing desire to understand the past and make its legacy accessible to future generations.

I remain committed to independent historical research, writing and the preservation of Bihar's cultural and spiritual heritage. For me, this work is not merely an occupation or a literary pursuit. It is a responsibility towards the memory of those who came before us and towards the generations who deserve to know the history, traditions and intellectual contributions that have shaped their region.

The journey continues, with the hope that careful research and sincere scholarship will help preserve the voices of the past for the future.
