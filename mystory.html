From Ancestral Memories to Historical Research: My Journey as a Researcher and Author

A journey that began with the stories of my ancestors in Sheikhpura, grew through questions about the past, and eventually found expression in historical research and books.

01 / The beginning

The Beginnings of My Research Journey

My journey as a researcher began when I was approximately seventeen years old. At that time, I was studying at SADN Convent School in Sheikhpura, Bihar, and living in my hometown, Sheikhpura.

My interest in history, however, had developed much earlier. During my childhood, I would often listen to stories about my great-grandparents, their contributions, their personal lives, and the places in which they had lived. These accounts, narrated by members of my family, gradually shaped my ability to listen attentively, comprehend historical narratives, retain information, and develop an enduring interest in the past.

What began as childhood curiosity eventually developed into a serious intellectual pursuit. I became increasingly interested in tracing my family's ancestry, understanding its historical connections, and documenting the heritage that had been passed down through generations. I realised that these stories were not merely family memories; they were fragments of a wider social, religious, and cultural history that deserved to be preserved.

I am documenting these accounts to introduce people to my ancestral background and to explain how the heritage from which I come has influenced my development as a researcher and writer.

02 / Ancestral roots

Ancestral Heritage and Family History

My ancestral history is closely associated with several historically significant families and places in present-day Sheikhpura and Lakhisarai districts of Bihar.

One of my distinguished ancestors was Hazrat Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari (Rahmatullahi Ta'ala Alaih), the grandfather of my grandfather. He was a renowned Islamic preacher, teacher, and spiritual guide. Among those associated with him was Rao Abdul Rahman Khan of Mircha, Jamui, whom he taught and guided.

Hazrat Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari was married in Urain, also known as Oren, a village in present-day Lakhisarai district. His father-in-law was Mir Irshad Hussain, popularly known as Gango Babu, a prominent zamindar of his time.

Mir Irshad Hussain belonged to the lineage of Hazrat Syed Shah Muhammad Yusuf Dulha Jajneri, through Hazrat Syed Shah Muhammad Jaan Jajneri (Rahmatullahi Ta'ala Alaih) of Urain.

On the other hand, my great-grandfather and I belong to the lineage of Hazrat Syed Shah Jamaluddin Jajneri through Hazrat Mir Syed Niyaz Ali of Pathraita, Sheikhpura.

Hazrat Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari had two brothers: Hazrat Mir Syed Sakhawat Hussain, who is buried at Pathraita Masjid, and Mir Syed Shuja'at Hussain, who donated the land for the construction of Pathraita Masjid. Both Mir Syed Sakhawat Hussain and Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari received their religious education from Madrasa of Firangi Mahal. They devoted their lives to Islam and its propagation.

The son of Maulana Mir Abdullah Bihari, Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz (Rahmatullahi Ta'ala Alaih), was also a Hafiz of the Holy Qur'an. He received his religious education from the Madrasa of Khanqah-e-Maulaiya, Maula Nagar, Surajgarha, Lakhisarai. As his childhood was spent in Urain, which is situated close to Maula Nagar, this institution became an important part of his educational journey.

He married the daughter of Abdul Hakeem, who was the son of Hazrat Mir Sarfaraz Hussain, the donor of land for Jamwara Masjid.

The descendants of Mir Syed Niyaz Ali held zamindari rights in the surrounding locality. In this context, Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz dedicated his khatiyani land as waqf property. This land subsequently became known as Hafiz Syed Abdul Aziz Qabristan, situated near the entrance of Jamwara village, Sheikhpura, close to the proposed Central School.

The family's historical journey reflects a remarkable movement across generations. The family, originally associated with Jamwara, migrated to Pathraita and subsequently to Urain following marriage and at the request of the father-in-law. After several generations, the family eventually returned to Jamwara, reconnecting with its ancestral roots.

A family's geographical journey
Jamwara → Pathraita → Urain → Jamwara

A movement across generations, shaped by family relationships, marriage, education and a return to ancestral roots.

03 / Education and service

My Grandfather and the Educational Legacy of the Family

The son of Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz and my grandfather, Hafiz Dr Syed Aashique Hussain, carried forward this family's religious and educational traditions.

He was married in Charyari, Sheikhpura, and eventually settled in Jamwara, the place from which our family's roots had originated.

Hafiz Dr Syed Aashique Hussain was a homeopathic physician as well as a Hafiz of the Holy Qur'an. He received his Qur'anic education from the same institution as his father, the Madrasa of Khanqah-e-Maulaiya, Maula Nagar, Surajgarha, Lakhisarai.

He passed away at the age of sixty-three in Jamwara and was laid to rest at Hafiz Syed Abdul Aziz Qabristan, near the grave of his mother.

He had five sons and two daughters. His sons included:

  1. Late Syed Ainul Huda, a journalist associated with The Times of India.
  2. Syed Sadique Hussain, a former employee of the Bihar State Madrasa Education Board.
  3. Late Syed Arif Hussain, a former teacher at Madrasa Jamia Habibia, Jamwara.
  4. Syed Shabbir Ahmad, a former teacher at Madrasa Jamia Habibia, Jamwara.
  5. Syed Ahmad Hussain Azizi, my father, former Secretary of Madrasa Jamia Habibia, Jamwara, and founder and former Headmaster of Madrasatul Banat Azizul Uloom, Jamwara, Sheikhpura.

My father established Madrasatul Banat Azizul Uloom in the memory of Hazrat Hafiz Mir Syed Abdul Aziz (Rahmatullahi Ta'ala Alaih), with the objective of promoting girls' education. The institution represents an important continuation of our family's commitment to religious learning and educational development. At present, my mother is serving as its In-charge Headmistress.

These generations of religious scholars, educators, social contributors, and individuals associated with public life have profoundly influenced my understanding of heritage. Their stories became an important source of inspiration and eventually encouraged me to undertake historical research and literary work.

04 / From memory to manuscripts

From Family History to Literary Research

As my interest in history grew, I began exploring subjects beyond my immediate family background. Eventually, following repeated encouragement from people close to me, I decided to write about twenty-five Muslim leaders, scholars, and other distinguished personalities from Bihar.

This became my first major literary undertaking, The Eternals of Bihar.

I subsequently worked on another book, Akhtar Orenvi: Bihar Mein Urdu Sahitya Ke Nirmata, focusing on Akhtar Orenvi, who also belonged to Urain. His stories and historical associations had remained in my memory since childhood.

However, after learning more about his religious affiliation with the Ahmadiyya community, I decided, for personal religious reasons, not to promote the subject through my publication. Consequently, I withdrew the book from publication.

Later, several friends encouraged me to undertake research on the history of Sufism in Bihar. Their suggestions introduced me to a subject that brought together many of my existing interests: historical documentation, religious traditions, the biographies of eminent personalities, regional heritage, and the preservation of cultural memory.

This eventually led to the writing of Bihar Aur Sufivad.

05 / The principal work

The Research and Publication of Bihar Aur Sufivad

The research and preparation of Bihar Aur Sufivad involved considerable effort, consultation, and support from several individuals.

Among those who extended valuable assistance during the research, promotion, and outreach of the book were my friends Muhammad Zabir Ansari, an International Karate Champion and Founder and Chairman of Zenith Social Foundation and the Institute of Dynamic Martial Arts; Syed Rushan Ali of Pind Sharif; Muhammad Abid Arman Hasan of Dhanbad; and Muhammad Farhan Raza Qadri of Deoghar.

Their encouragement and cooperation played an important role in helping the book reach a wider readership and bringing the history of Sufism in Bihar to public attention.

After completing the manuscript, I consulted several knowledgeable individuals to seek their suggestions and academic observations. Among them was Professor Dr Uzma Azhar Ali of Jawaharlal Nehru University, New Delhi. She offered suggestions that helped me improve the manuscript.

Following the necessary corrections and professional copy-editing, the manuscript was ready for publication.

I then approached Dr Muhammad Sajjad Alam Rizvi, who was kind enough to write the foreword to the book. His contribution added an important academic dimension to the publication.

Publication date
27 April 2025

The day Bihar Aur Sufivad was published.

Alhamdulillah, the book was finally published on 27 April 2025.

Since its publication, Bihar Aur Sufivad has received appreciation from scholars, religious personalities, administrators, writers, and readers from different backgrounds.

Among those who have expressed their appreciation are Hazrat Maulana Ghulam Rasool Balyawi, President of Idara-e-Shariah, former Member of Parliament in the Rajya Sabha, and former Member of the Bihar Legislative Council.

Muhammad Mudassir Ashrafi, a Khalifa of Maulana Syed Mahmood Ashraf Ashrafi and a PhD in Persian from Jamia Millia Islamia, also appreciated the work.

Professor Sunil Jaglan, founder of initiatives such as Gali Band Ghar and Bibipur Model, whose social initiatives have received appreciation from Prime Minister Narendra Modi, also wrote in praise of the book.

Similarly, Dr Muhammad Shahid Raza Khan, a 2019-batch IAS officer serving as Deputy Municipal Commissioner in the Bihar Government and an alumnus of Jawaharlal Nehru University and Al-Jamiatul Ashrafiya, Mubarakpur, Uttar Pradesh, also appreciated the publication.

These responses from people associated with academia, public administration, religious scholarship, and social development have been particularly encouraging.

06 / Reception

National Bestseller Recognition and the Book's Reception

Although the book was published on 27 April 2025, it became available for sale on 1 May 2025.

To my immense happiness, Bihar Aur Sufivad achieved Bestseller status on Amazon on the evening of its first day of availability. It also secured the fourth position in the Indian Writings category. Within a few days, on 4 May 2025, it reached the first position.

The response was encouraging and exceeded my expectations. Readers from different parts of the country began contacting me to obtain copies, and the book gradually reached a national readership.

The news of its sales and bestseller ranking was also covered by leading Hindi newspapers, including Dainik Bhaskar and Dainik Jagran.

For an independent researcher and young author working on the history of a relatively specialised subject, this response was particularly meaningful. It demonstrated that regional history, when documented and presented with dedication, can find an audience far beyond its geographical origins.

07 / Patna, 29 June 2025

The Official Book Launch in Patna

Following the book's publication and its encouraging reception, Muhammad Zabir Ansari, International Karate Champion and Founder and Chairman of Zenith Social Foundation and the Institute of Dynamic Martial Arts, proposed organising its formal launch under the banner of Zenith Social Foundation in Patna.

I accepted the proposal, and initially, 22 June 2025 was fixed for the programme. However, owing to certain circumstances, the date was subsequently rescheduled to 29 June 2025.

The programme was expected to bring together several prominent personalities from public administration, politics, religious scholarship, journalism, literature, and social service.

Among the distinguished guests expected to attend were IPS Vikas Vaibhav, then Inspector General of the Magadh Range, Bihar; Maulana Ghulam Rasool Balyawi, Chairman of the Bihar State Minorities Commission; Dr Rajan Singh, a Member of the DISHA Committee in Bihar who held ministerial rank; Ashutosh Kumar, political leader and founder of Bhumihar Brahman Ekta Manch and Rashtriya Jan Jan Party; Shakeel Hashmi, State President of the Minority Department of Hindustan Awam Morcha (Secular); and Md Asharul Haque, President and founder of Rashtriya Secular Majlis Party, which has a sitting MLA from the Bhangar constituency in West Bengal.

However, on the same day, an organisation in Bihar had called for protests against the Waqf Bill. Consequently, some of the expected guests were unable to attend.

Nevertheless, the programme was graced by several distinguished personalities, including IPS Vikas Vaibhav, Maulana Ghulam Rasool Balyawi, Shakeel Hashmi, and Md Asharul Haque.

Other notable attendees included journalist Junaid Hussain Khan Maneri, author Badal Bharti, Sufi researcher Syed Rushan Ali, poet Syed Wasif Alam Moazzami, and numerous other scholars, writers, intellectuals, and well-wishers.

The event became an important occasion for discussing the historical significance of Sufism in Bihar and the need to document the region's shared cultural and spiritual heritage.

08 / A memorable acknowledgement

IPS Vikas Vaibhav's Remarks on Bihar Aur Sufivad

One of the most memorable aspects of the book launch was the appreciation expressed by IPS Vikas Vaibhav.

While speaking about the significance of the publication, he observed that the book addressed an important gap in the documentation of Bihar's historical heritage. Referring to the scattered nature of historical information concerning several sites in the state, he drew a comparison with his own efforts to compile historical material relating to Maner.

On the book's contribution to historical documentation

“Syed Amjad Hussain Sahab Ki Jo Pustak Hai, Isse Bahut Bada Gap Fill Hoga. Bihar Mein Aise Bahut Se Sites Hain Jinke Baare Mein Aap Janna Chahen, Toh Uplabdh Samagrih Ek Sthaan Par Nahin Hai. Maner Ke Bare Mein Baad Me Maine Bhi Varsh 2014 Mein Ek Blog Likha, Jise Aap Internet Par Search Kar Sakte Hain, Milega Aap Ko, Usme Maine Jitne Bhi Sources Mile The, Unko Ek Jagah Compile Karna Shuru Kiya. Sab Se Pahle Haafiz S. Ahmad Ki Ek Lekh 1930 Ki Likhi Hui Mujhe Prapt Hui, Jo Patna University Mein Us Samay Professor The. Usse Achcha Lekh, Us Samay Tak Nahin Tha, Uske Baad Main Kah Sakta Hun Maine Jo Likha Maner Ke Upar Sab Ko Compile Karte Hue, Aur Usse Acha Lekh Agar Koi Ho Sakta Hai To Wo Syed Amjad Hussain Ji Ki Kitab, Bihar Aur Sufivad Mein Hai, Isme Bahut Sanchep Mein Hai. Kyonki Mujhe Isko Padhne Ka Avsar Mila.”

“The book written by Syed Amjad Hussain Sahib will fill a significant gap in historical documentation. There are many sites in Bihar about which people may wish to learn, but the available information is not collected in one place. Regarding Maner, I also wrote a blog in 2014, which you can find on the internet. I began compiling all the sources I could locate in one place. The first important piece of writing I came across was an article written in 1930 by Hafiz S. Ahmad, who was then a professor at Patna University. Until that time, I had not come across a better article on the subject. Subsequently, I also wrote about Maner by bringing together the available material. I can say that if there is a work that presents this subject even better, it is Syed Amjad Hussain Ji's book, Bihar Aur Sufivad. It brings together the material in a concise manner, and I say this because I had the opportunity to read it.”

While discussing the wider influence of Sufism in Bihar, IPS Vikas Vaibhav also referred to a speech I had delivered about one of my ancestors, Hazrat Syed Ahmed Jajneri, whose resting place is at Nadiawan.

He highlighted the remarkable fact that, despite the absence of a Muslim population in Nadiawan for many years, local people have continued to look after the dargah and mazaar. He considered this an example of how Sufi heritage transcends communal boundaries and remains part of a wider collective cultural tradition.

On Sufism as shared heritage

“Bihar Mein Aise Bahut Sare Sites Hai, Jise Dekhne Wala Us (Sufi) Parampara ka Nahin Hai, Lekin Sufism Mein Muslim Community Ke Sath Dusre Community Ka Bhi Judaao (connect) Raha.”

“There are many such sites in Bihar whose visitors do not necessarily belong to the Sufi tradition. Nevertheless, Sufism has maintained a connection not only with the Muslim community but also with people from other communities.”

Towards the conclusion of his address, IPS Vikas Vaibhav expressed his appreciation for the work and encouraged me to continue my research. His concluding remarks were particularly inspiring:

A message of encouragement to continue the journey

“Young Scholar Syed Amjad Hussain, Saamne Aate Hain, Research Karte Hain, Toh Definitely Aur Logon Ko Bhi Inspire Karenge Kyonki Ye Hamara Collective Heritage Hai. Agar Past Ki Baat Ham Nahin Samjhenge, Toh Present Ko Bhi Nahin Samjh Sakenge, Aur Future Banane Ki Baat To Bahut Kathin Hai. Apne Past Ko Achche Tarah Se Samjhna, Usme Nishchit Roop Se, Jitna Main Padh Saka (Bihar Aur Sufivad), It's a very invaluable contribution towards the history of Sufism in Bihar. Iske Liye Main Badhaayi Dena Chahta Hun Lekhak Syed Amjad Hussain Ji Ko, Aur Yaatra Yahin Na Ruke, Bahut Aage Badhe, Aur Aap Ham Log Achche Se Padhe, Aur Depth Mein Aap Le Ja Sake. Aapko Meri Dher Saari Shubhkamnaen.”

“When a young scholar like Syed Amjad Hussain comes forward and undertakes research, he will certainly inspire many others to do the same, because this is our collective heritage. If we do not understand our past, we cannot understand our present, and building our future becomes even more difficult. It is essential that we understand our past properly. From whatever I have been able to read of Bihar Aur Sufivad, I can say that it is a truly invaluable contribution to the history of Sufism in Bihar. I would like to congratulate the author, Syed Amjad Hussain Ji, on this achievement. May his journey not stop here, but continue much further. May he continue to study, explore, and take this subject to greater depths. I extend my very best wishes to him.”

His words were particularly significant to me because they recognised not only the value of the book but also the wider importance of historical research and the preservation of collective heritage.

09 / The road ahead

Continuing the Journey

Looking back, I find a clear connection between the stories I heard during my childhood, my family's ancestral history, my early curiosity about historical personalities, and my eventual decision to become a researcher and author.

My journey began in Sheikhpura, with stories narrated within the family. Those stories gradually became questions, the questions developed into research, and that research eventually took the form of books.

The Eternals of Bihar, Akhtar Orenvi: Bihar Mein Urdu Sahitya Ke Nirmata, and ultimately Bihar Aur Sufivad represent different stages of this intellectual journey.

The publication of Bihar Aur Sufivad, its national bestseller recognition, the appreciation it received from distinguished personalities, and the encouragement expressed at its Patna launch have all strengthened my determination to continue working in this field.

I remain conscious that the history of Bihar is far more extensive than what has so far been documented. Numerous personalities, institutions, religious traditions, historical sites, manuscripts, and local narratives still require systematic research and preservation.

As an independent researcher, my objective is to explore these lesser-documented aspects of history and make reliable historical information accessible to readers, particularly younger generations.

For me, historical research is not simply an academic exercise. It is also a responsibility towards the past and an effort to ensure that the heritage inherited from previous generations is not lost to time.

The journey that began with listening to the stories of my ancestors continues today through research, writing, documentation, and the preservation of Bihar's historical and cultural heritage.

A continuing commitment

From memory to history. From history to the next generation.

What began as an effort to understand my own ancestral background has become a wider commitment to documenting the people, places, institutions and traditions that form Bihar's shared historical inheritance.

“The stories of the past become history when we take the responsibility to preserve them.”
